Annual Neighborhood Meeting ▼

📍 Albuquerque Country Club, Banquet Room · 6:00 PM
Annual

2026 Huning Castle Neighborhood Annual Meeting — Minutes
Wednesday, May 13, 2026 · Albuquerque Country Club Banquet Room

📋 Attendance: Board members present: Brenda Marks (President), Rudy Garcia (Vice President), Kendall Corley (Treasurer), Anna Lamberson (Secretary), Lara Bryson (Communications), Robert Rodriguez, Joann Pinkalla, Mark Pinkalla, Jon Patterson and Lynn Hightower. 45 neighborhood members signed the attendance sheets.
📝 Overview

The annual meeting covered several topics of importance to the neighborhood and elected new members to serve vacant board seats. Presenters included Carl Christensen (ABCWUA), Commander J.L. Sanchez (APD Valley Area Command), Councilor Joaquin Baca (District 2), and Markella Clinton (Mayor's Office).

✅ Adoptions
  • Minutes for April were approved without changes.
  • New board members Clara Hargraves, Tony Gradia, and David Kleinfeld were elected by acclamation.
  • Current board members Brenda Marks, Rudy Garcia, and Joanne Pinkalla were retained by acclamation.
💧 Water Authority Programs & Rebates

Carl Christensen highlighted programs including a $20 rebate for attending live webinars, 25% off (up to $100) for tree care (planting, pruning, mulch), and the xeriscape program offering $3/sq ft for replacing grass with desert-friendly plants. Free irrigation and landscape consultations are available. Visit 505Outside.com or email Askanexpert@abcwua.org.

🏛️ City Council & Community Updates

Councilor Joaquin Baca discussed $3 million in capital outlay from the 2026 legislative session, ongoing budget priorities (transit, animal welfare, city employees), optimism for the downtown Business Improvement District, the likely teardown of the Lindy's building, the Wells Fargo housing conversion (underway and fully funded), potential closure of Silver Street Market, and a promise to review the Wendy's intersection near Central and Lomas for safety. The Rail Trail continues to build despite a $15 million loss of federal funds. Short-term rental legislation failed but will be reintroduced.

🔥 Safety & Emergency Preparedness

Rudy Garcia emphasized bosque fire preparedness — embers can fly as far as a mile. He encouraged neighbors to prepare go bags, know which neighbors need mobility assistance, and maintain fire-wise properties. The City has installed 48 new fire sensors (Montano to César Chávez) and drones for fire monitoring. Rudy volunteered joint home visits for perimeter checks.

🚔 Community Safety & Law Enforcement

Commander J.L. Sanchez reported on the effectiveness of Shot Spotter technology, citing the Forest Park incident where bullet casings tied into four cases citywide. He described a recent home invasion case involving a repeat offender and highlighted the role of ACS in handling mental health and non-emergency situations. The Valley Area Command covers a large area with 64 officers across three shifts. Report incidents via 311, 242-COPS, 911, or the Atlas 1 mobile app. Commander Sanchez's direct line: 505-252-2589.

📊 Association Updates
  • Treasury: $22,087.89 in the account (Kendall Corley).
  • Communications: Lara Bryson presented the new neighborhood website (huningcastlena.org) and encouraged residents to sign up for HCNA alerts.
  • Board: Outgoing board members Erika Harding, Lynn Hightower, and Anna Lamberson were thanked for their contributions.
🏠 Ordinance 26-14 & Homelessness Services

Markella Clinton explained Ordinance 26-14, which includes $500 fines for obstructing sidewalks and two-year safety zones with increased APD and ACS presence. Gateway West was selected for a safe outdoor space for unhoused individuals. Current transportation includes two Sun Vans and regular medical/veterinary transport. Job training and affordable housing preparation are available, with a focus on young adults pursuing education at CNM. Community feedback on the ordinance is encouraged.

📌 Action Items
  • Review ABCWUA programs at abcwua.org or contact Carl Christensen (cwchristensen@abcwua.org, 505-289-3026).
  • Councilor Baca will review the former Wendy's intersection for safety and reconfiguration.
  • Website (huningcastlena.org) will be updated with new board members and events.
  • Sign-in sheet will be used to update HCNA email alerts.
  • A credit card payment option will be added to the website for online donations.
  • Citizens are invited to provide feedback on Ordinance O-26-14.
  • Report emergencies to 911 and non-emergencies to 505-242-COPS.
  • Rudy Garcia will conduct joint home visits for fire preparedness.

Meeting called to order: 6:34 PM · Meeting adjourned: 7:58 PM

Alebrijes Installation at Robinson Park ▼

📍 Robinson Park, 8th & Central · All Day
Community

Eight giant 20-foot-tall alebrijes — fantastical, brightly colored sculptures inspired by Mexican magical realism — will be installed around Robinson Park at 8th and Central on Friday, May 22. Walk, run, or drive by to watch them go up!

🎨 What are Alebrijes? — Alebrijes are vivid, imaginative folk art sculptures that combine features of multiple animals, rooted in Mexican artisan tradition. These monumental fiberglass pieces are part of Fantastic Animals from Mexico: Alebrijes and Nahuales, a nationwide touring exhibit celebrating Mexico's rich cultural legacy. The eight lighted sculptures will be on display along Route 66 in downtown Albuquerque from June 1 through July 17, 2026.
📅 Key Dates
  • Friday, May 22: Sculptures installed at Robinson Park — come watch!
  • Tuesday, May 27: Press conference, 1:00 – 2:00 PM at Robinson Park
  • June 1 – July 17: Official public display period (lighted day and night)

Monthly Board Meeting ▼

📍 Albuquerque Country Club Board Room · 6:00 PM

Huning Castle Neighborhood Board Meeting — Minutes
Wednesday, June 10, 2026 · Albuquerque Country Club Board Room, 6:00 PM

📋 Attendance: Brenda Marks, Kendall Corley, Rudy Garcia, Clara Hardgrave, Lara Bryson, Jon Patterson, Mark Pinkalla (via Zoom), and Joanne Pinkalla (via Zoom). Markella Clinton from Mayor Keller's Office attended with Bobbie Cisneros, Deputy Director of ABQ Transit. Mayor Tim Keller was also in attendance.
📝 Call to Order

The meeting was called to order at 6:05 PM. A quorum was present. Minutes from the HCNA Annual Meeting held May 13 were adopted as written.

🚔 Crime Report — Rudy Garcia

Crime stats for May showed a slight increase due to a few situations of young adults coming off Central and congregating around Forest Park on three separate occasions, typically on a Friday or Saturday between 12 AM and 4 AM. On one occasion, shots were fired before police arrived; APD suspected the groups may have been street racers meeting up before racing.

Rudy worked with Commander Sanchez from Valley Command to set up more graveyard patrols in the area, and to have officers coming onto the graveyard shift do their briefings at the park. No further activity has occurred since, and the increased APD presence appears to be keeping the group away. Overall, the neighborhood continues to be very quiet, with a few "suspicious person" reports.

Duran's gift cards were given to Commander Sanchez and Carl Christensen in thanks for presenting at the May Annual Meeting.

🗳️ Board Elections — New Officers
  • President: Rudy Garcia (nominated by Brenda Marks, unanimous vote)
  • Vice President: Brenda Marks (nominated by Rudy Garcia, unanimous)
  • Secretary: Joanne Pinkalla (nominated by Brenda Marks, seconded by Rudy Garcia, unanimous)
  • Treasurer: Kendall Corley (retained by unanimous support)

Lara Bryson continues to run HCNA Alert communications, and Jon Patterson continues managing the website.

🗓️ Meeting Schedule & Bylaws

The board discussed amending the bylaws to allow meeting via email, virtually, or on scheduled months throughout the year, noting that HCNA is one of few boards in the city that meets monthly. If there is no new business or pressing matters, a meeting may be canceled, held virtually via Zoom, or conducted via email. The board is also open to meeting at local businesses in addition to ACC.

There was a brief discussion about a zoning request from the Garcia family to replat their properties into one lot; emails on the request have circulated among board members.

🍦 National Night Out — August 4

National Night Out is scheduled for August 4, 2026, in Albuquerque and across the country. The board noted great first-responder attendance in the past but lower neighborhood turnout, and agreed the event should continue to support APD and AFD while avoiding a conflict with the first day of school. Ideas discussed included promoting the event with art displays from neighbors, kids, and adults, and rebranding it as an "Ice Cream Social" to be more inviting — Brenda has arranged for The Brain Freezer Ice Cream Truck (Salena Allison) to attend. Date and format will be finalized at the July meeting.

🚌 Luminaria Tour — ABQ Transit Options

Mayor Tim Keller, acting as an unofficial liaison between the City and the neighborhood, presented ABQ Transit's proposed changes to the Luminaria Tour along with Markella Clinton and Deputy Director Bobbie Cisneros. Following feedback given after a March presentation to the board, ABQ Transit proposed three options — offered as talking points to start dialogue, not final decisions:

  • Option 1: Keep the tour as-is but reduce bus tours to four runs (5:30, 5:45, 6:15, 6:30), shortening the time the neighborhood is impacted by road closures.
  • Option 2: Convert to a walking tour — nine buses would shuttle tour-goers from parking lots to drop-off points at Raynolds and Kit Carson, running every 15–20 minutes from 5:30–7 PM, with visitors walking a suggested route and being picked up at the same locations. This would also discourage private vehicles from entering the neighborhood.
  • Option 3: Keep the same tour times but simplify and minimize the route, resulting in much less barricading. A bus would be available for mobility-impaired individuals.

Placards: Neighbors could request a placard for family or friends entering the neighborhood for holiday gatherings, allowing them past barricades under explicit rules, with pedestrian safety remaining the top priority.

ABQ Transit is open to suggestions and needs to begin promoting tour changes in July to give the public enough notice. HCNA will continue meeting with ABQ Transit to discuss options. Concerns raised included choke points, general traffic slowdowns, special groups such as cyclists on the tour route, and bathroom access for the walking-tour option. The board will discuss the options in detail at the July meeting.

Meeting called to order: 6:05 PM · Meeting adjourned: 7:00 PM

Monthly Board Meeting ▼

📍 ACC Board Room · 6:00 – 7:00 PM
Meeting

The HCNA Board met on July 8, 2026 in the ACC Board Room from 6:00–7:00 PM. A quorum was present, and the board adopted new meeting ground rules, approved a shift to meeting every other month, and reviewed neighborhood safety concerns.

⚠️ Safety Reminder — Car break-ins were reported on two separate days in early June in the Kit Carson/Alcalde area, along with an auto theft on Alcalde Place. Please keep vehicles locked and valuables out of sight. The Bosque fire remains under investigation; the body found does not currently appear to be associated with the fire.
📋 Attendance

Present: Rudy Garcia (President), Brenda Marks (Vice President), Lara Bryson (Communications), Fred Rivera, Jon Patterson (Webpage), Dave Kleinfeld, Clara Hardgrave, Robert Rodriguez, Mark Pinkalla, and Joanne Pinkalla (Secretary, attended virtually).

Also attending: Peter Rice (Downtown Area News) and neighbor Rachel Ribeiro.

📝 Summary
  • Adopted new ground rules to keep meetings on task, on time, and respectful.
  • Discussed the Bosque fire investigation and recent car break-ins/auto theft; residents urged to secure vehicles and property.
  • Approved an updated HCNA Purpose Statement.
  • Approved a change to meeting every other month instead of monthly, with additional meetings (in person, email, or Zoom) as needed for time-sensitive topics.
  • Decided to keep the Luminaria Tour route as-is with reduced bus hours; the city may provide access placards for residents on Christmas Eve.
  • Discussed possibly opening newsletter/webpage advertising to any business, with criteria to be developed to keep an appropriate neighborhood tone.
  • Confirmed National Night Out plans for August 4th, 6–7 PM at Forest Park.
  • Formed a subcommittee to explore HCNA liability insurance and amend the Bylaws.
🗳️ Motions & Decisions
  • June 2026 board meeting minutes unanimously approved.
  • Motion (Brenda, seconded by Lara) to adopt HCNA Purpose Statement #2 into the next Bylaws revision — approved.
  • Motion (David, seconded by Clara) to hold board meetings every other month — approved unanimously.
  • Motion (Brenda, seconded by Lara) to proceed with Option 1 of the city's Luminaria Tour options — approved unanimously.
✅ Action Items
  • Rudy & Jon — add a website tab with tips on securing vehicles/property from crime, using info from APD contacts.
  • David, Brenda & Rudy — amend Bylaws to include the approved HCNA Purpose Statement.
  • Rudy — inform ABQ Transit the neighborhood prefers to keep the current bus route with reduced hours for the Luminaria Tour, and request household placards.
  • Brenda — secure the Brain Freezer Ice Cream Truck for National Night Out.
  • Rudy — invite First Responders to National Night Out.
  • David, Brenda & Rudy — explore insurance options to protect HCNA.

HCNA account balance: $22,625.89
